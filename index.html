What a website might learn

Not everything is secretly available to a web page. Some information is shared as part of normal browsing; sensitive features require permission.

  • Connection detailsYour IP address is visible to the website’s server and may indicate an approximate region or internet provider.
  • Browser and device hintsBrowser settings and device characteristics can be combined to distinguish visits. Limiting tracking and using privacy protections can help.
  • LocationPrecise location requires your permission in the browser. Don’t approve it for a site without a clear reason.
  • Camera and microphoneA site must request permission to use them. A suspicious page has no good reason to ask unexpectedly.
  • Information you enterNames, passwords, payment details, and one-time codes can be exposed if you type them into a fake form.
  • Activity on the pageA site can observe interactions on its own pages. Be cautious with unexpected links, downloads, and urgent requests.

Pause before you trust a message

  • Check the sender and the real destination of links—watch for misspellings and look-alike domains.
  • Don’t share passwords, payment details, or verification codes through a link in an unexpected message.
  • Verify urgent requests using a known, official website or phone number—not the contact details in the message.
  • If you entered a password, go directly to the real service, change it, and turn on multi-factor authentication.
