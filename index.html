What a website still cannot read

Even with every permission, browsers enforce hard security limits.

  • Phone number & installed appsNot exposed. No browser API reveals your phone number or app list.
  • Contacts, SMS & call historyNot a web API. Only native apps with OS permission can access these — a pop-up claiming otherwise is always a lie.
  • Device serial numberNot provided to websites. At most, browsers share broad platform hints.
  • Full file systemWebsites can only access files you explicitly pick in a file dialog — not your whole storage.
  • Other websites you've visitedModern browsers prevent cross-origin history sniffing.
  • Camera without permissionAlways requires the native browser prompt. The danger is not the prompt — it's not reading the URL and trusting the wrong site.

🛡️ How to protect yourself

  • Urgency + big prizes = manipulation. Real companies never use countdown timers to pressure permission grants.
  • Check the URL first. Always verify the domain before tapping Allow. Fake domains look convincing (e.g. paypa1.com).
  • Camera = your face. The moment you grant camera access, a site can silently capture photos, as this demo just showed.
  • Location = your home address. Granting it to a fake site tells them exactly where you are.
  • No web page can read your contacts or call history. That claim is always a scam.
  • Close suspicious tabs immediately — you can always revoke permissions in your browser settings.
